# Helping an unclear aspiration become a useful plan

Treat onboarding as a collaborative conversation, not an intake exam. The goal is enough clarity
to take a meaningful next step, not certainty about the rest of the user's life.
Use [onboarding-decisions.md](onboarding-decisions.md) to track prerequisites, ready questions,
evidence and current shared understanding. Keep the map scoped to useful choices and allow
multiple branches to share constraints. An empty set of ready questions may require evidence or
deferral rather than a declaration that every decision has been resolved.

## Adaptive question sequence

Start broad: “What would you like to be different, even if you do not know how to get there?”
Reflect the user's answer in plain words and check the intended meaning. Then choose only the next
relevant questions from these categories; do not send the entire list as a form.
Ask one to three independent ready questions, then wait for their answers. A milestone question
whose answer depends on a route still under discussion belongs in a later round. Partial replies
leave unanswered questions open. Offer recommendations selectively with reasons and alternatives;
questions about personal ambitions and tradeoffs remain neutral and open.

| Need | Example question | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Meaning | “What would doing that look like in everyday life?” | Distinguishes a title, skill, experience or lifestyle |
| Motivation | “Which part of it appeals to you most?” | Routes can serve different underlying values |
| Starting point | “What relevant experience or preparation do you already have?” | Avoids an unnecessary restart |
| Constraints | “What should this fit around, and what tradeoffs would be unacceptable?” | Respects time, money, location and existing obligations |
| Uncertainty | “Which part feels hardest to figure out?” | Directs research or a low-cost experiment |
| Success | “What evidence would tell you this is moving in the right direction?” | Turns vague progress into observable signals |
| Choice | “Which route fits best, or would you prefer to explore before choosing?” | Keeps ownership with the user |

Offer examples only to help thinking, not to steer toward a preferred biography. A declined answer
is not permission to infer it from old conversations. Do not diagnose traits or assume incapacity.

When the user opts into [life-document context](life-documents.md), use selected captured evidence
to clarify preparation, constraints and opportunities. Check dates, extraction coverage and stale
or conflicting facts with the user. Also offer grounded project and atomic-note candidates; a
document need not contribute to the roadmap to contain useful knowledge. No inferred obligation
or recommendation becomes a chosen goal automatically.

## Route comparison card

For each plausible route: intended outcome; fit with user criteria; confirmed prerequisites;
unknown eligibility; effort and cost ranges with sources; dependencies; advantages; tradeoffs;
reversible first step; and evidence that would change the recommendation. If only one formal route
exists, say so rather than inventing alternatives; compare ways to prepare or test fit instead.

For regulated pathways, locate the relevant official authority after clarifying jurisdiction.
Distinguish gaining useful skills, being eligible to apply, earning a credential, and being authorized
to practice. These are different milestones. Do not promise admissions, hiring, approval or licensure.

## Synthetic worked conversation

User: “I want to contribute to open-source software, but I do not know where to begin.”

Assistant first clarifies whether the attraction is learning, collaboration, impact or experience;
asks about current familiarity and available time; then reviews supplied contribution guides or
requests cited findings from a separate research session. It compares documentation, issue reproduction and a small code change
without presenting code contribution as inherently superior.

If the user is unsure, propose an exploration: inspect two communities, try reproducing one issue,
and review which activity felt useful. Success is learning enough to choose a sustainable next step,
not “become a maintainer in three months.” Only after the user chooses should the roadmap proposal
include that exploration project and its few initial tasks. Other routes stay inactive.

## Stop conditions and quality checks

- Stop expanding options when the user has enough information to choose the next step.
- If overwhelmed, reduce the scope to one direction or one experiment.
- If no route is chosen, retain uncertainty explicitly; no invented deadlines or active backlog.
- If facts conflict, show the conflict and the next verification source; do not average incompatible rules.
- If research is unavailable, produce a research plan with unanswered questions, not fabricated conclusions.
- The final roadmap must be recognizable to the user as their chosen direction, in their own terms.
- Recap and confirm enough shared understanding for the chosen next step, with unresolved or
  deferred branches visible, before drafting adopted records. Exact record and per-note approvals
  are still required before those records enter the vault.
