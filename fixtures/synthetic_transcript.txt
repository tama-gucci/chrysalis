[00:00:02] Prof. Vance: Welcome back, everyone. Today we are diving deep into the core
safety invariant of the Raft consensus protocol: the Log Matching Property.

[00:00:18] If you recall from last Tuesday, we distinguished leader election from log
replication. Today I want to make an emphatic point about why Raft never requires
complex commit-rollback protocols like Paxos. It all hinges on one inductive invariant.

[00:01:05] Here is the formal definition: The Log Matching Invariant states two things.
First: If two entries in different logs have the same integer index and the same term,
they store the exact same state machine command. Why? Because a leader creates at most
one entry with a given index in a given term, and leader logs are append-only.

[00:02:14] Second: If two entries in different logs have the same index and term, then
their logs are identical in all preceding entries, from index 1 up to index N.

[00:03:01] Student Question: Prof. Vance, how does the leader enforce that second property
during AppendEntries RPCs?

[00:03:15] Prof. Vance: Excellent question. The leader includes the term and index of
its immediately preceding entry in the AppendEntries RPC—prevLogIndex and prevLogTerm.
If the follower doesn't find a matching entry in its local log with that exact index
and term, it rejects the RPC. This is the consistency check. When the follower accepts,
it guarantees induction holds.

[00:04:45] Therefore, commit safety is guaranteed: once a leader commits an entry by
replicating it on a majority of servers in its current term, that entry is guaranteed
to be present in the logs of all future leaders.
